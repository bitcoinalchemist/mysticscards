Build your understandingBirth cards, readings, cycles, and connections

What is cardology?

Cardology uses a standard deck as a symbolic language for exploring personal themes and cycles.

A birthday identifies a birth card. Its suit and rank offer a starting point; its place in the wider arrangement connects it with other cards. Mystics Cards brings traditional correspondences together with original explanatory writing.

Readings offer ideas for reflection, not scientific findings or certain predictions.

Your birth card

Enter a day and month in Finder. Begin with the card’s suit and rank, then explore its full reading and the other cards connected with it.

Planetary Ruling Cards

A Planetary Ruling Card (PRC) connects a birthday’s zodiac sign with the playing card associated with that sign’s planetary ruler. The birth card and PRC are related but distinct parts of the reading. In Finder, choose Tropical or Sidereal to view the corresponding sign and ruling card. Scorpio shows both Mars and Pluto as rulers.

Birthdays near a sign boundary may also show a cusp alternative. Solar Time needs the birth year, time, and place. It estimates longitude from the selected time zone, so treat results near solar midnight as approximate.

The Life Script

The Life Script is the seven-card reading linked to a birth card. Each card occupies one of seven planetary positions, from Mercury through Neptune. Each position adds a theme to the reading, and the cards are also considered together as one pattern.

Finder presents the Life Script with the birth card’s reading. In the Spreads chart, those same seven cards are highlighted within the wider 52-card arrangement.

Displacements

A displacement is a relationship between two cards: one occupies the place associated with the other in the Life Script pattern. The displaced card remains part of the pattern; the cards are read together to consider how their themes meet.

Finder shows the cards a selected card displaces and the card that displaces it. Fixed cards hold their own place and have no separate displacement partner.

Comparing two people

Add a second birthday in Finder to explore connections between two readings. Shared cards and chart positions offer different themes to consider together; they do not determine the outcome of a relationship.

Cycles through time

Cycles follows the changing card pattern across five horizons: 13-Year, 7-Year, Yearly, 52-Day, and Daily. Each horizon has its own sequence. Select a period and move the viewed date to see which card is active at that point in the timeline. Cycles needs the actual birth year; day and month alone do not determine a person’s age.

Chart mechanics & correspondencesQuadration, calendar, and piano-note correspondences
Quadration and the 52-card chart

Quadration is the repeatable rule used to arrange the 52 cards in the chart. Start with Natural Order—Ace through King in Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, then Spades—and number the positions from 1 to 52. These position numbers are Solar Values. They label places in the chart; they are not times. Applying the same mapping repeatedly produces each successive arrangement.

Solar Time is a separate Finder tool. It uses the birth date and clock time with a time-zone reference longitude to estimate the local solar day at birth. Near solar midnight, that date can differ from the calendar date and produce a different birth card.

The mapping is a permutation: every position maps to one position, so no card is lost or duplicated. Its movements form six cycles:

  • Three fixed points: J♥, 8♣, and K♠ stay in place.
  • Two alternating pairs: 2♥ and A♣ exchange places; 9♥ and 7♦ exchange places.
  • One 45-card cycle: the other 45 cards move through the same positions before returning to their starting places.

The cycle lengths account for the deck: 52 = 45 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1. The full arrangement repeats after LCM(45, 2) = 90 applications. This describes the card movement mathematically; symbolic meanings are a separate layer of interpretation.

Cards and the calendar

In this calendar symbolism, the four suits are associated with the year’s four quarters, and the 13 ranks in each suit with the weeks of a quarter. Together they give the deck a 52-part structure for thinking about the year.

This is a symbolic correspondence, not a literal calendar calculation: 52 weeks make 364 days, while a solar year has extra days. Cardology traditions differ in how they account for that difference.

Piano note correspondence

Each card is assigned one white-key note in the standard 88-key piano range. Follow the Spiritual Spread order from A♥ through 10♠ to map 49 cards down from B7 to C1. The three crown cards are J♠ at C8, Q♠ at B0, and K♠ at A0. Together, the assignments use all 52 white keys once; Finder Details shows the note for the selected card.

Practical questionsBirth dates, contacts, Solar Time, and historical readings
What birthday information do I need?

Day and month identify a birth card. Personal Cycles also needs the birth year; load a saved contact with a complete birthday. Solar Time needs the full date, clock time, and a birthplace or time zone.

Birth dates and card assignment

Finder assigns a Solar Value from the month and day using 55 − (2 × month + day); a birth year is not needed for that lookup. December 31 has Solar Value 0 and maps to the Joker, which sits outside the 52-card sequence.

February 29 maps to Solar Value 22. For age-based Cycles, when a year has no February 29, the date calculation treats March 1 as the birthday boundary. Save a complete valid calendar date when a tool needs the birth year.

How are contacts saved?

Contacts are saved in this browser only. Export them to back up or move to another device. Clearing this site’s browser data can remove saved contacts. Import merges contacts with the existing list.

How precise is Solar Time?

Solar Time uses the selected time zone’s reference longitude, rather than the exact birthplace longitude. Treat results near solar midnight as approximate. Solar Values are different: they number card positions in the chart and are not times.

Yenlo and The Mystic Test Book

Yenlo opens historical card readings drawn from Olney H. Richmond’s 1893 book, The Mystic Test Book, including its Grand Spread when one is available for the selected card. The wording and symbolism belong to Richmond’s source material and may differ from the site’s original readings; some cards have no transcribed entry.
